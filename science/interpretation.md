# What positional uncertainty does to quantum information

A block of $`n`$ labelled qubits can carry $`n`$ coherent qubits without noise. Completely removing the positional labels by a uniform permutation twirl does not destroy all quantum information: the largest surviving coherent representation has dimension $`n+1`$. It therefore still carries $`\log_2(n+1)`$ logical qubits.

The surprising change occurs when **each physical carrier has any fixed positive probability of being replaced or erased**. Under independent replacement noise, the two-way quantum capacity of the entire scrambled block obeys

```math
Q_2\le f_n(q)\le\log_2K_n(q)\le-\log_2q.
```
For flagged erasure the exact value is $`f_n(q)`$, and its limit reaches $`-\log_2q`$. Thus the constant ceiling is a real information-theoretic boundary for the model, not a consequence of a weakly chosen code.

## The mechanism

Positional twirling destroys the multiplicity subsystems and inter-sector coherence but leaves the spin representations. Every spin-$`j`$ representation can be generated by a symmetric $`2j`$-qubit state with singlet padding, so the receiver's most useful coherent register is no larger than the largest symmetric block. Independent erasures remove a binomial number of particles. A symmetric block of $`m`$ particles retains, conditional on $`\ell`$ losses, only $`\log_2[(m+1)/(\ell+1)]`$ ebits of optimally distillable resource entanglement per use.

For fixed $`q>0`$, the typical loss count is proportional to $`n`$. The ratio $`(n+1)/(L+1)`$ therefore tends to $`1/q`$, rather than growing with $`n`$. The proof works for **all physical encodings**, not just the symmetric code used to attain the erasure rate. Unflagged replacement is no more informative than flagged erasure, and a CP-order comparison gives the stronger finite-protocol fidelity inequality.

## Assistance makes a qualitative difference

For erasure, classical feedback lets Bob announce which loss count occurred and use reverse hashing. This gives the limiting rate $`-\log_2q`$ for every fixed $`0<q<1`$.

Without free classical feedback, the unassisted rate is

```math
\lim_{n\to\infty}Q
=\max\biggl\{0,\log_2\frac{1-q}{q}\biggr\}.
```
At $`q\ge1/2`$, the ordinary quantum capacity is zero even though two-way-assisted entanglement distribution remains positive for every $`q<1`$. Both rates refer to complete blocks; the rate per carrier vanishes as $`n\to\infty`$ at fixed $`q>0`$.

## Noise strength and block size do not commute

If the replacement probability vanishes with block size as $`q_n=c/n`$, the expected number of losses remains finite and the exact erasure rate still grows like $`\log_2n`$. This does not contradict the constant ceiling at fixed $`q`$: the hypotheses of the two limits differ. The scale $`nq`$ governs the crossover.

## Quantum and classical information part company

Even after fixed depolarization, a family of product states with distinct Bloch vectors supports at least $`\frac32\log_2n-O(\log\log n)`$ reliably distinguishable classical bits per block. A compression argument gives an upper bound $`2\log_2n+O(1)`$. Coherent quantum communication, by contrast, remains $`O(1)`$. The separation is about **operationally different resources**, not an assertion that classical encodings are optimal or that quantum mechanics gives no classical-message advantage.

## Boundary of the result

The theorem concerns fresh unrecorded full permutations, qubits, independent identical local channels with a positive replacement component, and no protected metadata or free quantum bypass. It does not prove a capacity law for one hidden permutation reused over time, for correlated noise, for arbitrary qudit Schur sectors, or for rank-deficient channels lacking a replacement decomposition. The exact formulas are for **flagged erasure**; the unflagged depolarizing capacity is bounded, not determined.
